## WaveForge (2025)

WaveForge was my final thesis project at university, where I explored one of the more interesting areas of procedural generation: **Wave Function Collapse**.

The idea behind the project was to create a playground where users can experiment with generating images using WFC algorithms. Users can upload their own images or image sets as input, choose between different approaches such as the **Simple Tiled Model** and the **Overlapping Model**, and generate completely new images based on the provided patterns.

The application also allows users to create their own pixel art directly inside the editor and use it as the foundation for generation. After creating something interesting, they can download their results, or share it on the app together with the input and configuration settings.

The sharing system was an important part of the idea: instead of only creating an image and moving on, users can share the settings behind the generation. Others can then take those configurations, experiment with them, and create their own variations.

Basically, the goal was to turn a complex algorithm into something people can play around with and use creatively.

The project was very well received during my thesis presentation. Both the idea and the execution were evaluated as **excellent** by the judges, which was a great confirmation that the concept was not only interesting on paper but also worked well as a finished product.

- Turned a theoretical concept into an interactive web application.
- Received excellent feedback for both the concept and the final implementation.
- Learned a lot about procedural generation, user experience, and designing tools for creativity.
